# 1. Choose the stack

## Goal

Pick the language that tells the right story for *this* evaluator, not the
language that makes you fastest.

## Default rule

If the brief is language-free and the company runs a standard stack, use that
stack.

Delivering a solid service in the company's language is a stronger signal than
delivering the obvious service in the language you have used for twenty years.
The cost is real: you will move slower and you must keep the crash-course bar.

## Confirm it

Do not guess. Ask the recruiter, or read the brief twice.

Write down:

- What every team there actually ships (languages, HTTP kit, store, CI)
- Whether the take-home *asks* you to use AI, allows it, or forbids it
- Why you are not using your home language, in one paragraph

That paragraph goes into `CLAUDE.md`. The agent will otherwise "helpfully"
steer you back to TypeScript, Python, or whatever dominates its training mix.

## When to stay in your home language

Stay if any of these is true:

- The brief names a language
- You cannot finish a crash course before the deadline
- You cannot review the output to the bar in step 0
- The company stack is unknown and nobody will answer

A weaker submission you can defend beats a fluent-looking submission you cannot.

## Stop condition

`CLAUDE.md` contains a stack decision with a reason, and you have confirmed
the AI policy.

## Do not

- Pick a new language as a stunt if the brief already names one
- Pick the company's language and then hide behind "the agent wrote it"
- Change language halfway through because generation got awkward
